# Demand Forecasting — Analyst Runbook

A retail planner wants per-(store, item, date) unit-sales forecasts that borrow strength across stores and product families, not one isolated time series per SKU. The dataset is a Favorita-style grocery sample — 3 stores, 25 items, and ~27,000 historical daily sales. The analysis builds a heterogeneous graph linking each sale to its store, its item, and the item's family, then trains a graph neural network (GNN) to regress unit sales, propagating signal through those store and product neighborhoods while the sale date drives temporal causality.

```text
3 stores · 25 items · ~27,375 daily sales  →  heterogeneous graph: Sale→Store, Sale→Item, Item→ItemFamily
      │
      ▼
/rai-predictive-modeling + /rai-predictive-training
   • GNN regression predicting Sale.unit_sales, with Sale.date as the temporal feature
   • message-passing over store, item, and item-family neighborhoods
   • forward 60-day test window                  -> test RMSE ~7.4 units per sale
      │
      ▼
   • weekly forecast per (city, item family): predicted vs actual unit sales
```

Each prompt is pasted into a fresh agent session loaded with the named `/rai-*` skill (named at the start of each prompt). They run in order in a single session — the predictive step reads the `Store`/`Item`/`Sale` concepts the build step created, and writes forecasts back. (This trains a GNN on a GPU predictive engine — a prerequisite.)

---

## 1. Build the ontology

**Prompt:** /rai-build-starter-ontology Build an ontology from `data/favorita_mini/stores.csv` (each store has a city, state, type, and cluster), `data/favorita_mini/items.csv` (each item has a family, class, and perishable flag), and `data/favorita_mini/sales.csv` (one row per store-item-date with unit sales and an on-promotion flag). Derive an item-family concept from the item families.

**Response:** Loads `Store` (3, with city/state/type/cluster), `Item` (25, with family/class/perishable), a derived `ItemFamily` (from the distinct item families), and `Sale` (~27,375 rows, each with `unit_sales`, `date`, and `onpromotion`, linked to its store and item).

## 2. Examine the ontology

**Prompt:** /rai-querying What concepts and relationships does the ontology have, and how many rows are in each?

**Response:** Four concepts — 3 `Store`, 25 `Item`, the derived `ItemFamily`, and ~27,375 `Sale` (one per store-item-date) — the history the forecaster learns from.

## 3. Train the demand forecaster

**Prompt:** /rai-predictive-modeling + /rai-predictive-training Train a graph neural network to predict each sale's unit sales (a regression). Build a heterogeneous graph where each sale connects to its store and its item, and each item connects to its family, so the model can borrow signal across stores and product families; feed the sale date as the temporal feature. Train on the history and forecast unit sales over a forward 60-day test window, writing the predictions back to the ontology.

**Response:** A regression GNN trains over the Sale→Store, Sale→Item, Item→ItemFamily graph (features include store city/type/cluster, item family/class/perishable, on-promotion, and the sale date). On the forward 60-day test window it reaches a **per-sale test RMSE of about 7.4 units**; predictions are written back per sale. (Exact RMSE varies with training; the order of magnitude and the weekly accuracy below are the stable read.)

## 4. Read the forecast

**Prompt:** /rai-predictive-training How do the forecasts compare to actuals when rolled up to weekly demand per city and item family?

**Response:** Aggregating per-sale predictions to weekly totals by city and item family tracks actual demand closely through the test window — e.g. Cuenca bakery and beverages forecasts move with the real weekly swings, with the largest gaps on holiday-spike weeks (early December). The weekly-aggregate RMSE is about 150 units per (city, family, week); the model captures the seasonal shape, and the graph lets sparse items lean on their family's and store's patterns.

## Data

Bundled CSVs in `data/favorita_mini/`: 3 stores, 25 items, ~27,375 daily sales (generated by `generate_favorita_mini.py`). The test-window length (60 days) and training config are constants in the script. Full model in `demand_forecasting.py`.
